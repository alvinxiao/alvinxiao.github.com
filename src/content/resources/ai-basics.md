A customer email needs a reply. You know what to say, but finding the words takes longer than you would like. A generative AI tool can help produce a first draft. You still need to check what it says.

That distinction is a useful starting point. Generative AI creates text, images, code, and other content using patterns learned during training and the context supplied to it. It can produce a convincing answer without knowing whether that answer is true.

## What it can help with

For a small business, useful experiments often involve work you already understand:

- Turn your rough notes into an outline for a newsletter.
- Suggest alternative wording for a product description.
- Organize a list of ideas into themes.
- Draft questions to ask before starting a project.

These tasks give you something to react to. You can spot missing details, choose a better phrase, or reject the result. Start where you can judge the output yourself.

## Why a fluent answer can be wrong

An AI tool can invent a fact, quotation, source, or policy. This is often called a hallucination. Asking it to be accurate does not remove that risk. A second answer from the same tool is not an independent fact check.

If a draft mentions your opening hours, compare them with your actual schedule. If it cites a source, open the source and check that it supports the claim. Verify calculations with a calculator or spreadsheet.

For medical, legal, financial, or other consequential decisions, get the relevant qualified advice. A polished explanation does not establish expertise.

## Give it a task you can inspect

A useful instruction describes the job, the audience, the information to use, and the desired format. For example:

> Write two friendly versions of a reminder email for a fictional pottery class. The class starts at 10am on Sunday. Ask attendees to arrive ten minutes early. Keep each version under 80 words. Use only these details and do not add a cancellation policy.

Compare the drafts with the supplied facts. Did the time change? Did the tool invent a location? Does the message sound like something you would send? Edit it before using it.

## Choose what information to share

Use fictional examples for your first attempts. Do not paste customer records, passwords, payment information, or confidential documents into a tool just to test it.

Data handling depends on the provider, product, plan, and settings. Check the current terms before using business information. A paid subscription alone does not establish that a tool is suitable for sensitive work.

## AI is one option

A reusable template might solve the email problem without generating a new answer each time. A spreadsheet formula might be more reliable for a calculation. Choose the approach that produces a result you can trust and maintain.
