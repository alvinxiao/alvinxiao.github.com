Generative AI produces text, images, or other outputs based on patterns learned from examples. A conversational tool can be useful for drafting or exploring ideas, but a confident answer is not evidence that an answer is correct.

## Begin with a small, low-risk task

Try asking for an outline, alternative wording, or questions to help you think through a topic you know. Keep the task small enough that you can review the result yourself.

Use invented examples rather than customer records, passwords, or private business information. Review a tool's data policies before putting real information into it.

## Describe what a useful answer looks like

Give the tool a purpose, an audience, and a format. For example, ask for three draft versions of a short reminder email using fictional details. Then edit the result to match what you actually mean.

## Check before you use

Check facts, calculations, quotations, and sources independently. Treat generated content as a draft you are responsible for reviewing, not as a professional determination or a guarantee.

## Know when another tool is enough

A template, a spreadsheet formula, or a clearer process may solve the problem more reliably. The goal is useful work, not using AI for its own sake.
