In this exercise, you will create and check a short reminder email for a fictional class. You need access to a conversational AI tool. No business records or customer details are needed.

## 1. Set a clear target

Use this fictional brief:

- Business: Cedar Street Pottery.
- Event: a beginner pottery class on Sunday at 10am.
- Request: attendees arrive ten minutes early and bring an apron.
- Tone: friendly and straightforward.
- Length: fewer than 80 words.

The target is a usable reminder with all the supplied details and no invented policies. Keep the brief beside the draft so you can compare them.

## 2. Ask for a first draft

Copy this instruction into the tool:

<div class="practice-prompt" data-copy-prompt="First draft">

> Draft a reminder email from the fictional business Cedar Street Pottery for a beginner pottery class on Sunday at 10am. Ask attendees to arrive ten minutes early and bring an apron. Use a friendly, straightforward tone and fewer than 80 words. Use only the facts in this instruction. Do not invent an address, booking link, price, or cancellation policy.

</div>

You should receive a short email draft. The wording will vary between tools and attempts. A different answer is not necessarily a better answer.

## 3. Check against the brief

Read the draft before asking for revisions:

- Does it say Sunday at 10am?
- Does it include arriving ten minutes early and bringing an apron?
- Has it invented any details?
- Is it under 80 words?
- Would you be comfortable sending that wording?

Check the word count yourself if the limit matters. If the draft is incorrect, fix the facts directly or ask for a revision and check again.

## 4. Make one focused revision

Try this follow-up instruction:

<div class="practice-prompt" data-copy-prompt="Revision">

> Make the wording more conversational. Keep all the class details unchanged. Remove promotional language and keep the email under 80 words.

</div>

Compare the new version with the first one. Recheck the facts as well as the tone. A request to improve the wording can change details you wanted to keep.

## 5. Compare the whole effort

Consider the time spent writing the instruction, checking the answer, and editing it. Compare that with writing the message yourself or adapting an existing template.

Keep the method if it helps. If it creates more work, reduce the task or use a template. You do not need to make the experiment succeed to learn something useful.

Before using a similar process with real inquiries, review the tool's data policies and decide what information it is appropriate to share. Keep a person responsible for the final message.
